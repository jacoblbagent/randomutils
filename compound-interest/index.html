About this tool

How the figure is worked out

The balance is stepped forward one month at a time: it grows by one twelfth of the annual rate, then the monthly contribution is added. Compounding monthly rather than yearly makes a small difference at the same nominal rate, because interest starts earning interest sooner.

The closed form for the same model is P(1+i)^n + C((1+i)^n − 1) / i, with i the monthly rate, n the number of months and C the monthly contribution. The table is the same loop, kept in src/tools/compound-interest/compound.ts.

What it leaves out

  • Tax on interest, account fees and fund expense ratios. Each of those lowers the real result.
  • Inflation. The figure is in today’s dollars only if you subtract inflation from the rate first.
  • Variable returns. A fixed 8% is a planning assumption, not a forecast for any particular investment.
  • Deposits that change over time. For a rising contribution, add it as a second run.
